Patch the Planet: global response coordination as agents uncover critical vulnerabilities
Loading...
Over the past two months, we led a coalition of maintainers, researchers, and security providers to
address critical vulnerabilities in PHP, an open-source programming language used to run websites
and web applications. Calif identified a critical flaw using GPT‐5.6 Sol as part of our Patch the
Planet(opens in a new window) initiative, with Wiz also independently discovering and reporting both
vulnerabilities. PHP’s maintainers led the fixes and release, with our teams and partners like Wiz,
Cloudflare, and others contributing research, technical support, and coordination to prepare
protections ahead of disclosure.
Given the severity of these flaws and PHP’s widespread use, we consider this one of the most
impactful security issues in recent memory. It highlights how AI can uncover security flaws with
internet-scale impact, but the same capabilities can also help attackers rapidly develop offensive
tools and exploits.
To our knowledge, frontier AI found these vulnerabilities before attackers did, opening a Defender’s
Window for maintainers and security providers to prepare fixes and protections ahead of disclosure.
To help more defenders use these capabilities, we’ve committed $1 billion in subsidized Daybreak
access through Daybreak for Frontline Defenders, focused on organizations with limited security
resources protecting essential services.
As significant as they are, these bugs are among several significant vulnerabilities we’ve already
found and are working to fix, and we expect similar findings to increase sharply in the coming
months. Drawing on our experience thus far, we’re sharing a proposed approach, appropriate for the
AI era, to responding to critical vulnerabilities in widely used software, and we’re asking the
security community to help improve this framework. It brings together our models, security
expertise, and maintainers to help turn findings into protection and build a response that can scale
as AI capabilities advance.
Coordinating a global response
Getting a fix into use can mean adapting it for older releases, preparing provider mitigations, and
updating systems across many organizations. A repeatable process helps those teams act quickly as
more vulnerabilities are discovered.
Through Patch the Planet, we contributed security expertise and model access to support the PHP
response, and worked with maintainers and research partners to test fixes and prepare guidance. We
also helped coordinate with infrastructure providers on protections for affected systems and engaged
government cyber agencies to help guidance reach critical organizations. This supported the
maintainer-led response and helped other defenders prepare.
For some findings, researchers can work directly with the software’s maintainers to investigate the
issue and develop a fix. Others need a wider response because they affect many products or because
distributors and service providers need time (which may be in short supply if frontier capabilities
are not too far ahead of those that are broadly available) to prepare updates and protections. In
those cases, the proposed framework can help maintainers identify who needs to be involved and
coordinate the work. The response can involve five stages:
Validate and fix the upstream vulnerability. The maintainer, researchers, and essential technical
partners confirm the finding, assess its impact, and develop and test a fix. This process includes
adversarial testing with frontier models to look for ways around the patch and related variants of
the vulnerability.
Give affected organizations time to act. We propose a framework for considering which organizations
need advance information to prepare protections, while maintainers decide whom to involve. We help
with outreach, information-sharing agreements, and coordination between teams. A distributor might
adapt a fix for an older release, a hosting provider might test a mitigation, and a security
provider might prepare detection or prevention measures, including web application firewall (WAF)
rules.
